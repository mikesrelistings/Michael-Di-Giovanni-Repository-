# Find the Estates First: An AI Playbook for an Antique Buyer

Prepared by Michael Di Giovanni for a friend who buys antiques out of homes and resells them. No website or tech background needed to start.

---

## 1. The one move: team up with a real estate broker

When a long-time owner dies or moves to care, the family has two problems at once: the house and everything in it. A broker handles the house. You handle the contents. Every estate lead is worth something to both of you, so trade them.

**How it works with Michael:**
- Michael already runs PropStream, a property data service, with monitored lists that email him when new properties match his filters. PropStream has a **Pre-Probate** filter that flags homes where an owner on title has a death record. PropStream says the point is to find the property before probate starts.
- Michael sets up a Pre-Probate monitored list on your ZIP codes. New matches hit his inbox and he passes along the contents opportunities.
- Free second layer: the **Estate Sale Radar** in this repo, which scans Cook County tax bill records for the paper trail that follows a death or a move to care (see `estate-sale-radar/README.md`).

**Rules before any money moves (talk to counsel first):**
- Illinois license law bars a broker from paying anything of value to an unlicensed person for real estate referrals (225 ILCS 454/10-15; Ill. Admin. Code 1450.780). Illinois REALTORS legal hotline guidance reads "anything of value" broadly, from cash to TVs to baseball tickets. So Michael does not pay you for house leads.
- If you pay Michael for contents leads while he represents the seller, that raises disclosure and conflict questions. Michael clears any arrangement with his managing broker and an attorney before it starts.
- Default: no money changes hands. Each of you just sends the other good business, and the family always picks who they hire.
- Not verified: whether PropStream's terms allow sharing its data with a non-subscriber. Michael checks before forwarding lists. If it's a problem, you get your own seat.
- Any marketing piece that names Michael as a broker needs the Real logo and the words "Real Broker, LLC" in the text.

## 2. Where estate leads come from, earliest first

| Source | Rough timing | Cost | How to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **People the family calls first:** estate and elder law attorneys, senior move managers, assisted living move-in coordinators, estate sale companies, clean-out crews, realtors | Earliest | Free | Become their go-to buyer. Estate sale companies often need someone to buy out a whole house or take what didn't sell. |
| **Obituaries** | Days | Free | Know what's happening in your area. Don't contact the family that week. |
| **PropStream Pre-Probate** (through Michael) | Before probate is filed, per PropStream | Michael's PropStream (PropStream says no extra cost; confirm in his account) | Respectful letter to the property or the family's mailing address. |
| **Probate court filings** | Weeks | Free | The Cook County Clerk's online docket includes probate cases; the Clerk says new filings can take a few days to post. Probate Division: 312-603-6441. |
| **Estate Sale Radar** (this repo) | Weeks to months | Free | Monthly list of homes where the tax bill passed to a spouse or child, moved away from the house, or is addressed to an estate. |

Never ask a funeral home, hospital or care facility for names of people who died or moved. It's a fast way to lose the relationships that matter most.

## 3. How to reach out without being the vulture

- **Mail, not cold calls or texts.** Calling or texting numbers from a skip-traced list brings federal TCPA and Do Not Call exposure. Letters don't. If phone outreach is ever on the table, get compliance advice first.
- **Tone:** PropStream's own guidance says to treat these leads with sensitivity, and a 2026 RISMedia piece advises messaging that is informative and supportive, never urgent or opportunistic. Offer help, not a pitch.
- **Timing (my recommendation, not a rule):** one letter, then one follow-up about 60 days later. Stop if they say no.
- **Send it where the family is.** When the radar shows the tax bill moved, mail to the new mailing address, not the empty house.

### Sample letter (your name, your business)

> Dear [Name],
>
> I'm [Your Name], owner of [Business], a local antique buyer serving [Area] for [X] years.
>
> When a family is settling a home, the contents can feel like the hardest part. I can help with that whenever you're ready, whether it's one piece of furniture or the whole house. I come out at no cost, tell you honestly what has value and what doesn't, and make a cash offer with no pressure. Many families get a second opinion, and I encourage it.
>
> If now isn't the time, please keep this letter. If I can help, call or text me at [Phone].
>
> With respect,
> [Your Name]
> [Business] | [Phone] | [Website or Google listing]

## 4. AI tools that pay off first, in this order

1. **Google Business Profile (free).** Google says creating a profile is free, and businesses that visit customers in person qualify. This is how "antique buyer near me" and "estate buyout" searches find you. Have AI write the description and your review replies. Ask every seller for a review.

2. **Identify on the walkthrough.** Snap a photo and ask Claude or ChatGPT: "What is this, who made it, what era, what marks should I check, what drives the value?" Then confirm the price with **eBay sold listings** and **WorthPoint**, a database of past sale prices (App Store listing: $26.99/month or $249.99/year; check worthpoint.com for current pricing). AI gets you oriented fast. It is not an appraisal and it can be wrong.

3. **List in minutes with eBay's Magical Listing.** Take a photo in the eBay app and it drafts the title, category, item details and a suggested price. A bulk version in Seller Hub turns batches of photos into drafts at once. Review every draft before posting; it can misidentify items.

4. **Track every piece in one Google Sheet.** Columns: Item, Photo link, Bought from, Date, Cost, Listed on, List price, Sold date, Sold price, Fees, Profit, Days held. Once a month, paste it into Claude and ask: "What sells fastest, what sits, where's my margin, what should I stop buying?" That's how you learn what to pay at the next house.

5. **Short videos of the finds.** 30 to 60 seconds: "What I found in a 1920s attic." AI writes captions. These bring in buyers and sellers ("call me before you clean out Mom's house"). Get the family's OK before filming in a home, and keep addresses, family photos and personal papers off camera.

6. **Letters to referral partners.** Have AI draft a short, personal intro letter to 20 local estate attorneys, senior move managers and estate sale companies. Mail it, then stop by with a card.
